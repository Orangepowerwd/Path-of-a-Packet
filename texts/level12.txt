How Packets Reach a CDN
A Content Delivery Network (CDN) uses geographically distributed Points of Presence (PoPs) and edge servers to deliver content closer to users. DNS may direct a browser to an appropriate CDN edge location. If the requested content is cached (cache hit), the edge server delivers it directly; otherwise (cache miss), it retrieves the content from the origin server. CDNs reduce latency, distribute traffic, and reduce load on origin servers.
